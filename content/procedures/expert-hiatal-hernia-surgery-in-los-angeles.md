## What is a Hiatal Hernia?

A hiatal hernia occurs when some part of your stomach pushes through your diaphragm into the chest cavity. The condition is experienced by millions of Americans and can lead to severe pain, among other health concerns, if left unchecked.

There are two main types of hiatal hernias:

  * **Sliding Hiatal Hernia (Type I):** Most frequent type, with the stomach sliding through the hiatal hole in an up-and-down fashion
  * **Paraesophageal Hernia (Type II-IV):** High-risk type in which part of the stomach is trapped above the diaphragm

## Hernia Symptoms That May Indicate You Need Surgery

  * ✔ **Severe Heartburn & Acid Reflux:** Disrupts sleep and significantly affects eating habits.
  * ✔ **Chest Pain:** High-severity symptom that can often mimic heart attack symptoms.
  * ✔ **Difficulty Swallowing:** A critical issue that can make eating uncomfortable or impossible.
  * ✔ **Regurgitation of Food:** Often leads to social embarrassment and nutritional concerns.
  * ✔ **Shortness of Breath:** Severely limits physical activity and daily performance.
  * ✔ **Feeling Full Quickly:** Increases the risk of unintended weight loss and malnutrition.

## Hiatal Hernia Surgery FAQ

### **1\. Is hiatal hernia surgery covered by insurance?**

Yes, when medically necessary, hiatal hernia surgery is typically covered by most insurance plans, including Medicare and Medicaid. Our team will help verify your coverage and work with your insurance provider.

### **2\. How long does the surgery take?**

Most laparoscopic [hiatal hernia repairs](https://healthylifebariatrics.com/what-size-hiatal-hernia-needs-surgery/) take 2-3 hours. Complex or revision cases may take longer.

### **3\. What are the risks of surgery?**

As with any surgery, there are risks including bleeding, infection, and anesthesia complications. However, laparoscopic hiatal hernia repair has a very low complication rate (less than 5%) when performed by experienced surgeons.

### **4\. Will I be able to eat normally after surgery?**

Yes, most patients can return to a normal diet within 4-6 weeks. Initially, you'll progress from liquids to soft foods, then gradually add regular foods back.

### **5\. Can hiatal hernias come back after surgery?**

Recurrence rates are very low (less than 5%) when surgery is performed properly. Our techniques focus on durable repair to minimize recurrence risk.
