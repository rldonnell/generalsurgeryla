## Common Symptoms of Appendicitis

### Abdominal Pain

Pain often begins near the **belly button** and then shifts to the **lower right abdomen**, becoming sharper over time.

### Nausea and Vomiting

Digestive upset commonly follows the pain, with **nausea, vomiting, and loss of appetite** as inflammation progresses.

### Fever

A **low-grade fever** may develop, while a higher fever can indicate a more advanced infection.

### Loss of Appetite

A sudden **loss of interest in food** and a general feeling of being unwell are common signs.

### Abdominal Tenderness

The **lower right side of the abdomen** may become tender, and pain often worsens with walking, coughing, or sudden movement.

### Digestive Changes

Some patients also experience **bloating, constipation, diarrhea, or a sense of abdominal fullness**.

## Diagnosis of Appendicitis at General Surgery LA

Appendicitis is inflammation of the appendix, a small pouch attached to the large intestine. It usually happens when the appendix becomes blocked, irritated, and infected. Once the appendix becomes inflamed, symptoms can progress quickly.

At **General Surgery LA**, we use a careful, modern approach to diagnose appendicitis and determine the most appropriate treatment plan. The goal is to make a clear and timely diagnosis so treatment can begin before complications develop.

Diagnosis may include:

### Clinical Examination

A thorough **physical examination** helps identify the location and severity of pain and assess for signs of abdominal irritation.

### Medical History

Reviewing the progression of **pain, nausea, fever, and bowel changes** helps distinguish appendicitis from other abdominal conditions.

### Blood Tests

Blood work can reveal **elevated white blood cell levels and inflammatory markers** that support the diagnosis.

### Imaging Studies

**Ultrasound or CT imaging** may be used to confirm appendicitis and evaluate for rupture or abscess formation.

## Benefits of Laparoscopic Appendectomy

The operation is performed under general anesthesia. Small incisions are made in the abdomen, and a [laparoscopic camera](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/laparoscopy/) is inserted to provide a magnified internal view. Specialised instruments are then used to carefully separate and remove the appendix.

Once the appendix is removed, the area is inspected, and the small incisions are closed.

Whenever appropriate, laparoscopic surgery offers several important advantages.

### Less Post-Operative Discomfort

Many patients experience **less pain after surgery** compared with a traditional open incision.

### Faster Recovery

Recovery is often quicker, allowing patients to return to **normal daily activities sooner**.

### Shorter Hospital Stay

Many patients can go home **the same day or after a short overnight stay**, depending on the severity of the case.

### Better Visualization

The **HD laparoscopic camera** provides a detailed view, supporting precise diagnosis and treatment.

### Minimal Aesthetic Impact

Small incisions are placed strategically to achieve an **effective result with minimal visible scarring**.

### Reduced Risk of Infection

**Smaller incisions** reduce exposure to external contaminants and may lower the risk of post-operative complications.

## Recovery After Laparoscopic Appendectomy

Recovery varies, but many patients return to light daily activity within a short period. More advanced cases, especially ruptured appendicitis, may require a longer recovery.

Recovery depends on how advanced the appendicitis was before surgery, but many patients recover well with appropriate post-operative care.

### Immediately After Surgery

Patients are monitored as the anesthetic wears off, and **early walking is encouraged** to support circulation and recovery.

### Returning to Eating

**Fluids are usually introduced first**, followed by light food as tolerated to help the digestive system adjust comfortably.

### Activity Restrictions

Light activity is encouraged, but **heavy lifting and strenuous exercise** should be avoided for a short period.

### Pain Management

Mild to moderate soreness is common and can usually be managed with **appropriate pain medication**.

### Incision Care

Keeping the **small laparoscopic incisions clean** is important for proper healing and optimal scar appearance.

### Follow-Up Care

A scheduled follow-up visit helps review healing, check the incision sites, and confirm that recovery is progressing well.
