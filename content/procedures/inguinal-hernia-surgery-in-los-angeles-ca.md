## What is Inguinal Hernia?

An **inguinal hernia** occurs when a portion of the intestine or fatty tissue pushes through a weak spot or tear in the lower abdominal wall, typically in the inguinal canal located in the groin. This condition often results in a visible bulge that may become more prominent or painful when coughing, lifting heavy objects, or straining.

While some inguinal hernias are present at birth due to a natural weakness, many develop over time because of increased abdominal pressure combined with aging or physical exertion. If left untreated, these hernias can lead to serious complications, such as **strangulation**, where the blood supply to the trapped tissue is cut off, requiring immediate emergency surgical intervention.

### Causes of Inguinal Hernia

Inguinal hernias develop due to a **weakness in the abdominal wall** near the groin. Key triggers include heavy lifting, chronic coughing, and straining during bowel movements.

### Risk Factors

Factors like aging, obesity, and previous surgeries can weaken the groin muscles over time. While some are present at birth, most form due to repeated abdominal pressure.

### Common Symptoms

Typical signs include a **visible bulge in the groin** that may worsen when standing. Patients often feel pressure, burning, or a dull ache at the site.

### Warning Signs

**Sudden, severe pain** in the groin accompanied by nausea or vomiting is a critical warning sign. This may indicate a strangulated hernia requiring immediate emergency care.
