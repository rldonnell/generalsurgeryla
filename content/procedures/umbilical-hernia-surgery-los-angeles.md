## What Is an Umbilical Hernia?

The belly button is a natural weak spot. It is where the umbilical cord attached before birth, and the tissue that closes over it in adults is thinner than the rest of the abdominal wall. When pressure inside the abdomen rises again and again, a small opening can form and tissue can push through it.

You may feel a soft bulge at or just above the belly button that is more noticeable when you stand, cough or strain, and that flattens when you lie down. A surgeon can usually confirm an umbilical hernia with a simple exam.

### Causes and Risk Factors

Umbilical hernias are more common after **pregnancy**, with **extra abdominal weight**, with a **chronic cough**, with **straining or heavy lifting**, and after **previous abdominal surgery**.

### Common Symptoms

Typical signs include a **visible bulge at the belly button**, a dull ache or pressure at the site, and discomfort that is worse with activity or at the end of the day. Some umbilical hernias cause no pain at all.

### Warning Signs

A bulge that becomes **hard, painful, or cannot be pushed back in**, skin over the hernia that turns red or dark, or nausea and vomiting can mean tissue is trapped. That needs **emergency care the same day**.

## Do All Umbilical Hernias Need Surgery?

Not right away. In adults an umbilical hernia does not close on its own, but a small, painless hernia that stays the same size and goes back in when you lie down can often be watched with regular check-ups.

Repair is usually recommended when the hernia:

* Causes pain or pressure
* Is getting bigger
* Limits your work, exercise or daily activity
* Has become hard to push back in

## How Dr. Moein Repairs Umbilical Hernias

The right repair depends on the size of the opening, whether you have had a hernia repaired before, and your general health.

### Small-Incision Repair at the Belly Button

For smaller hernias, the repair is done through a small incision hidden at the belly button. The tissue is placed back inside the abdomen and the opening is closed with stitches, often reinforced with a small piece of mesh.

### Laparoscopic Mesh Repair

For larger hernias, hernias that have come back, or patients who have had abdominal surgery before, Dr. Moein often uses a **laparoscopic repair**. A camera and instruments go in through a few small incisions, and mesh is placed under the opening to reinforce the abdominal wall.

### Why Mesh Is Often Used

Repairs closed with stitches alone are more likely to come back, especially for larger openings. Mesh reinforces the weak area and lowers that risk. Read more in our article on [mesh vs non-mesh hernia repair](/hernia-mesh-vs-non-mesh-repair-2026/).

## Umbilical vs. Inguinal Hernia

An umbilical hernia forms **at the belly button**. An [inguinal hernia](/inguinal-hernia-surgery-in-los-angeles-ca/) forms **in the groin**, where tissue pushes through the lower abdominal wall. Both are common, both tend to grow over time, and both are usually repaired with minimally invasive techniques. Dr. Moein explains the difference in the second video on this page.

## Umbilical Hernia Surgery Recovery Timeline

**1\. Day of Surgery (Days 1 - 3)**

Most patients go home the same day. Some soreness at the incisions is normal and is managed with the medication prescribed by our team. Walking is encouraged from the first day.

**2\. Early Recovery (Week 1)**

Most patients move from prescription pain medication to over-the-counter medication and return to desk work within about a week. Avoid lifting anything heavy.

**3\. Intermediate Recovery (Weeks 2 - 4)**

Light activity resumes. Continue to avoid heavy lifting, pushing and pulling while the repair heals.

**4\. Full Recovery (4 - 6 Weeks)**

Most patients return to full activity, including lifting and core exercise, after receiving clearance from **Dr. Moein**. Patients with physically demanding jobs may need more time off, and this is planned at your consultation.
